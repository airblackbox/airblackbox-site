Design Principles
Five decisions that define AIR Blackbox

Every architecture choice serves one goal: compliance evidence that you own, you control, and you can defend under audit.

01

Local-first. Always.

Every audit record is written to your filesystem, your database, your infrastructure. Nothing phones home. Nothing requires an internet connection. If our website goes offline tomorrow, your compliance evidence is unaffected.

02

No API key required

pip install air-blackbox and you're running. No signup, no account creation, no vendor relationship. The scanner, the trust layers, the audit chains - all work without authenticating to any external service.

03

You own the cryptography

HMAC-SHA256 runs in your process, on your machine. The signing keys never leave your environment. You can verify the chain yourself with standard tooling - no proprietary verification endpoint needed.

04

Open source. Auditable code.

Every line of code across all 11 packages is on GitHub under permissive licenses. You can read the signing logic, the policy engine, the scanner rules. No black boxes inside the blackbox.

05

Framework-native integration

Trust layers hook into LangChain callbacks, CrewAI decorators, OpenAI SDK, Claude Agent SDK, and Google ADK. One import, one line of config. No separate system to maintain.

06

Scanner + runtime, not just one

Static analysis catches gaps in your code before deployment. Runtime trust layers capture evidence during execution. You need both to satisfy Articles 11 and 12. Most tools only offer one.

Cryptography
Proven standards vs. exotic algorithms

Some tools market post-quantum signature schemes as a feature. Here's why that's a distraction for compliance evidence in 2026.

HMAC-SHA256 (AIR Blackbox)

NIST approved Yes - FIPS 198-1
Used by Banking, healthcare, AWS, Stripe
Runs where In your process
Dependencies Python stdlib (hashlib)
Auditor familiarity Universal
Quantum threat (2026) Not applicable to HMAC

Post-Quantum Schemes (e.g. ML-DSA)

NIST approved FIPS 204 (Aug 2024)
Used by Research, early adopters
Runs where Vendor's servers
Dependencies Vendor's cloud API
Auditor familiarity Very low
Quantum threat (2026) Decades away for symmetric

The technical reality: HMAC-SHA256 is a symmetric construction. Grover's algorithm (the quantum threat to symmetric crypto) would require a 2128 quantum search - well beyond any projected quantum computer capability. NIST's own guidance is that HMAC-SHA256 remains secure in a post-quantum world. Post-quantum signature schemes solve a different problem (public-key authentication) that isn't relevant to tamper-evident audit chains. Marketing post-quantum crypto for compliance logs is solving a problem that doesn't exist.

Regulatory Reality
What auditors actually ask for

We've read the regulation. Here's what Articles 11 and 12 actually require - and what they don't.

Art. 11 - Technical Documentation

Requires documentation of the AI system's design, development, and intended purpose. AIR Blackbox's static scanner checks your codebase against 48 requirements and generates evidence bundles. No SaaS needed.

Art. 12 - Record-Keeping

Requires automatic logging of events during operation, with records that enable traceability. The key word is tamper-evident - you must prove records haven't been modified. HMAC-SHA256 chains do exactly this.

What's NOT required

The EU AI Act does not prescribe specific cryptographic algorithms, does not require post-quantum signatures, does not require cloud-based signing services, and does not require any vendor relationship for compliance evidence.
