EU AI Act Article 9: building a risk management system you can prove.

If you run a high-risk AI system, Article 9 is the one that does not let you file a document once and move on. It asks for a living risk process, kept current across the whole lifecycle, and the burden is on you to show you actually did it. Here is what it requires, who it covers, and how to leave evidence behind instead of a promise.

What Article 9 actually requires

Article 9 of the EU AI Act (Regulation 2024/1689) requires providers of high-risk AI systems to establish, implement, document, and maintain a risk management system. The key word is maintain. It is not a report. It is a continuous, iterative process that runs across the entire lifecycle of the system and is reviewed and updated on a regular basis.

In practice the process has to cover four things:

  1. Identify and analyze the known and reasonably foreseeable risks the system can pose to health, safety, and fundamental rights when used for its intended purpose.
  2. Estimate and evaluate the risks that can emerge both in intended use and under reasonably foreseeable misuse.
  3. Account for post-market data, evaluating other risks that surface once the system is live, based on monitoring.
  4. Adopt targeted risk management measures until the remaining residual risk is judged acceptable, including testing against defined metrics before the system goes to market and as it changes.

The regulation also calls for particular attention where a system could affect minors or other vulnerable groups. The throughline is simple: risk work is never finished, and every round of it is supposed to be recorded.

Who has to comply

Article 9 applies to providers of high-risk AI systems. That includes the Annex III use cases: AI used in hiring and employment decisions, lending and creditworthiness, education and exam scoring, access to essential public and private services, law enforcement, migration, and biometric identification. If your model decides something that materially affects a person's life or rights, assume you are in scope until you have confirmed otherwise.

The deadline moved, the preparation did not. The obligations for Annex III high-risk systems now apply from December 2, 2027 under the EU's revised timeline. That is not breathing room. A continuous risk process that has only existed for a month before an audit is not a continuous risk process. The teams that look credible in 2027 are the ones building the record now.

The hard part: continuous, and you have to show it

Most teams can write a risk assessment. The trap in Article 9 is the gap between doing the work and being able to prove the work. When a regulator, an enterprise customer's procurement team, or your own board asks how risk was managed over the last year, a single PDF dated last week answers the wrong question. They are asking for the process: what risks you flagged, when, what you changed in response, and whether anyone could have quietly edited that history after the fact.

That is an evidence problem, not a writing problem. You need a dated, reviewable, tamper-evident trail that shows the risk process ran continuously, not a snapshot assembled the week before the audit.
